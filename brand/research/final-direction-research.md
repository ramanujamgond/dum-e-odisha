# Dum E Odisha — final identity research

Reviewed 15 October 2026. Scope: seven designer-published food identity case studies. These are references for design principles, not assets to reproduce. This is not local competitor research, trademark clearance, a consumer study, or evidence that any particular style increases sales. Studio descriptions of results are not independently verified.

## Decision

The name must read **Dum E Odisha**. The immediate problem is word recognition: the existing flowing “dume” lettering and continuous pot lid obscure the boundary between Dum and E. Changing the colours alone will not resolve it. Build one clear, original wordmark with an unmistakable gap between **DUM** and **E**, and give **ODISHA** enough size to belong to the name. Preserve warm food character through restrained shapes, colour, and supporting packaging graphics.

The recommendation below is our synthesis of the published cases and this project's brief; the studios did not prescribe this solution for Dum E Odisha.

## What the primary sources actually show

### 1. Jaani Maani Biryani — Bombay404

The studio describes a delivery-first biryani identity that moved away from generic dish imagery toward illustrated scenes of communal eating. Indian comics informed the illustration language, while street-stall typography informed customised lettering. The logo and narrative packaging form related but distinct parts of the system. This is a useful Indian biryani reference because the brand idea extends beyond a cooking-pot symbol. [Studio case study](https://www.bombay404.com/work/jaanimaanibiryani)

**Transfer:** let the main mark carry the name clearly; put fuller food storytelling on sleeves, bags and social layouts. Do not borrow its lettering or characters. For Dum E Odisha, a restrained original rice/aroma illustration is enough; dense storytelling should not be stuffed into the logo.

### 2. Raunaq — TAL Creatives

The studio traces its system to Punjab, architectural geometry and agricultural colours. It explicitly balances decorative display typography with clean supporting type. Modular patterns extend across packaging and restaurant surfaces; colour coding and type preserve consistency while dishes retain individual expression. [Studio case study](https://theartlogic.com/work/branding-visual-identity/)

**Transfer:** establish roles rather than merely listing colours and fonts. The expressive mark identifies the business; clean type identifies the dish, portion or ordering information. Cultural motifs must be tied to a truthful brand story. Punjab-specific motifs are not appropriate evidence for Odisha, and should not be transplanted.

### 3. PizzaUP — Pentagram

Pentagram pairs a bold wordmark with horizontal lines. Crucially, the “UP” component is raised and underlined, becoming a distinct, reusable unit within the name and messaging. The identity uses a simple kit of elements across menus, packaging and environmental graphics. [Studio case study](https://www.pentagram.com/work/pizzaup/story)

**Transfer:** a short name component can be differentiated structurally. Dum's final m and E need an actual gap; colour can reinforce that gap but must not be the only separator. Borrow the principle of an independently readable unit, not PizzaUP's raised-and-underlined execution.

### 4. Super Peach — Pentagram

The identity combines hand-drawn wordmarks and illustrations with ABC Repro as a functional supporting typeface. A black-and-white palette keeps the wider system coherent while expressive artwork carries personality. It is shown across menus, bags, stationery and uniforms. [Studio case study](https://www.pentagram.com/work/super-peach)

**Transfer:** professional food branding does not require extra colours or a complicated emblem. Contrast between an expressive name and disciplined information can supply sophistication. Dum E Odisha can retain two warm colours while keeping supporting copy quiet and legible.

### 5. Urban Tadka — Neat Work Studio

The studio designed an identity and packaging for a contemporary Awadhi kitchen selling through delivery apps and selected grocers. It describes a bright, playful system intended to stand out in both contexts. Packaging cut-outs reveal food, and the physical product supports boil-in-bag use. [Studio case study](https://neatworkstudio.com/project/urban-tadka/)

**Transfer:** test the identity as a tiny app avatar and on an actual delivery container. A beautiful large presentation board is insufficient. Dum E Odisha should have a simpler no-tagline version for small formats and a prominent full name on the bag. Do not claim packaging materials or preparation features that the business has not confirmed.

### 6. Biryani Blues — 4Design

The studio describes a refresh spanning identity, characters, packaging, store design, communication and social media while retaining Hyderabadi references. This illustrates the breadth of a coherent restaurant identity rather than a single decorative logo. [Studio case study](https://www.4design.co.in/case-studies/biryani-blues)

**Transfer:** use the same name lockup, colour roles and tone on the logo sheet, bag, bowl sleeve and guide. Mockups must not quietly invent a new wordmark on each object. Avoid unverified heritage claims and invented founding dates.

### 7. India Gate biryani masala — ICARUS

The studio explains its task as celebrating regional biryani diversity while keeping a coherent India Gate family. Regional palettes, illustrations and typography distinguish variants under that shared structure. [Studio case study](https://icarus.co.in/work/indiagate)

**Transfer:** Dum E Odisha can offer multiple biryani flavours without recolouring the core logo for every dish. Place variant names and a limited accent on packaging labels while the parent identity stays stable. “Odisha” is the brand name; it should not imply a specific traditional recipe unless the menu supports that claim.

## Recommended final direction and checks

Use a confident rounded wordmark: **DUM** in deep chilli, **E** as a separate saffron element, **ODISHA** on its own clearly readable line. Keep DUM and E visually related through stroke weight and baseline, with unmistakable negative space between them. An original compact handi/aroma emblem may sit above or alongside, but no line should bridge the word boundary. Avoid making the E look like an ampersand, superscript, rice grain or decorative flourish.

Use rice cream as the main substrate, chilli as the dominant brand ink, saffron as a deliberate accent, and near-black for practical information. These are design roles, not press specifications. Flat artwork should have no gradient or faux metallic effect. A monochrome version must retain the same word separation. Keep **“Flavour in Every Layer.”** below the logo in larger applications, and omit it where it becomes unreadable.

Build one realistic takeaway scene with a paper carrier, lidded biryani tub and sleeve/seal. Repeat the same artwork faithfully. Keep dish naming separate from the business name: “BIRYANI” may identify a product or category but must not re-enter the official logo as “Dum E Biryani Odisha.” Show a clean logo proof beside the mockup so texture, perspective and lighting cannot disguise weak lettering.

Review exact spelling, the DUM/E gap, name hierarchy, monochrome recognition and reduced-size legibility. Raster generation can establish the final visual direction but does not establish editable vectors, exact spot colours, manufacturing tolerances or print readiness. Those require a production artwork stage and a physical proof.
