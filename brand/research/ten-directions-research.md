# Dum E Odisha: research for ten illustration-led identities

Research date: 15 October 2026. Scope: primary-source craft references and practical translation into ten **different contemporary identity concepts**. The original traditions and the proposed AI artwork are distinct. All symbols and meanings suggested below are our design synthesis, unless explicitly attributed to a source.

## Four Odisha references: what changes between them

### 1. Saura-inspired community cooking

**Source observation.** Odisha's craft-development agency identifies Saura painting with the Saura community in Rayagada and Gajapati, describing abstract human/animal figures, geometry, natural elements and ritual significance. Its Lanjia Saura account identifies Idital/Ikon and ancestral worship. These are culturally situated practices, not a generic label for any white geometric village drawing. [SIDAC — Crafts of Odisha](https://sidac.odisha.gov.in/en/crafts-of-odisha)

**Design synthesis.** An original scene of two or three abstract figures preparing a shared pot can communicate participation and hospitality. Give the handi the dominant silhouette; let elongated arms and a rice-grain rhythm create motion. Keep thin narrative figures in larger secondary illustrations and simplify the small symbol. Use clay, rice white and a small seed-yellow accent. Label the result **contemporary Saura-inspired cooking illustration**, not an authenticated traditional Idital. Do not transplant a ritual composition or call the user's white-on-red stock reference Saura without evidence.

### 2. Pattachitra-inspired storytelling

**Source observation.** The Government of Odisha describes cloth preparation, border-first composition, flat white/red/yellow/black colour areas and fine black finishing lines. Religious, mythological and folk subjects coexist in its account; Raghurajpur is associated with the craft. [Government of Odisha — Patta Chitra](https://odisha.gov.in/en/odisha-tourism/patta-chitra), [Odisha Tourism — Arts, Crafts & Handlooms](https://odishatourism.gov.in/en/arts-crafts-handloom)

**Design synthesis.** Build a secular food story: a single cook or pair of hands tending a rounded handi, with bold black contour and very few decorative curls. Use a deliberately framed composition, red ochre, yellow and ivory. The packaging can tell the longer cooking story in bordered panels; the logo should not attempt a miniature painting. No deity, temple seal or implied sacred-food status is needed. Label it **Pattachitra-inspired contemporary illustration**.

### 3. Pipili appliqué-inspired patchwork

**Source observation.** SIDAC describes Chandua as cut coloured cloth shaped into birds, animals, flowers, leaves and decorative motifs, then hand-stitched onto a cloth base. Odisha Tourism records Pipili's established appliqué practice, including festive textiles. [SIDAC — Crafts of Odisha](https://sidac.odisha.gov.in/en/crafts-of-odisha), [Odisha Tourism — Pipili](https://odishatourism.gov.in/en/places/pipili)

**Design synthesis.** This direction should look assembled from large, interlocking colour patches rather than fine painted outlines. A crescent pot, three leaf-shaped steam patches and a scalloped apron can form an energetic food symbol. Use indigo, vermilion and warm yellow on cream. Enlarge a simple stitch rhythm on the bag; keep stitch detail out of tiny symbols. Do not simulate handmade provenance: this is a digital, **Pipili appliqué-inspired identity**, not an artisan-produced textile.

### 4. Jhoti Chita-inspired rice and steam

**Source observation.** The Government of Odisha documents white rice-paste linework on walls and floors, made using fingers or a twig with cloth. It links the practice with domestic observances and harvest, including paddy imagery. It separately describes coloured-powder Muruja; the two media should not be conflated. [Government of Odisha — Jhoti, Chita, Muruja](https://odisha.gov.in/en/odisha-tourism/jhoti-chita-muruja)

**Design synthesis.** Make the material connection to biryani explicit: one continuous rice-white stroke forms a pot and branches into paddy-like steam. Use leaf green, rice white and a restrained earthy accent. The line should feel warm and deliberate, with dots/grain marks used sparingly. Avoid tracing a worship-specific arrangement. Present the concept as **Jhoti Chita-inspired rice-line illustration**, with a secondary all-solid version for small reproduction.

## Six other distinct concepts

5. **Shared Table — contemporary folk illustration.** Two broad human silhouettes lean around a common handi; their arms create a welcome. Use flat cut-paper-like masses, terracotta, dark teal and cream. Unlike route 1, this is a compact expressive emblem rather than a line-drawn narrative scene. It makes no specific traditional-art attribution.
6. **Dum Garden — Mughal botanical inspiration.** A rounded cooking vessel grows into a spare symmetrical plant of rice ears and aromatic leaves. The Met records observed flowers and invented hybrid botanical forms in Mughal albums. Use deep plum, muted saffron and parchment; detailed floral borders belong on large packaging. Describe this accurately as **Mughal botanical-inspired**, a courtly art reference rather than tribal folk art. [The Met — Botanical Painting: Composite Flower](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/838376), [The Met — Flower Studies](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/825613)
7. **Lift the Lid — modern flat symbol.** A lifting lid and two warm hands create a welcoming open vessel. Large flat shapes, coral, ink and cream; no folk claim. Meaning comes from revealing aroma and serving generously.
8. **Hidden Aroma — negative space.** A strong oval/circular pot silhouette contains a clear rice-grain or steam cutout. One clever cutout is enough; avoid hidden details that fail at 32 pixels. Dark aubergine and marigold, with cream knockout. Test the symbol alone before adding the name.
9. **Aroma Spiral — geometric study.** A controlled sequence of rounded arcs suggests rising aroma and layered rice above a vessel. Use moss, brass yellow and warm ivory. Golden-ratio proportion is an optional construction method, not proof of design quality. If generated raster geometry has not been measured, label it **golden-ratio-inspired geometric study**, never “precisely constructed at φ”. An exact claim would require a documented vector construction and measured ratios; do not draw decorative Fibonacci circles after the fact and present them as evidence.
10. **The Dum Host — illustrated character.** An original friendly cook uses arms and apron to frame a handi, with a confident silhouette that works without a detailed face. Rust, warm navy and pale butter. Avoid copying the supplied moustache/chef references; develop a different pose, silhouette and negative-space structure.

## What to learn from Kruti Coffee

Kruti's own account connects its business to coffee growers in Kindiriguda, Odisha and describes its farm-to-cup offering. This is its documented story. [Kruti Coffee — About Us](https://kruticoffee.com/pages/about-us)

Our inference is to connect imagery to a credible story about place, cooking and hospitality, then repeat the visual grammar consistently on packaging. Do not copy Kruti's logo, slogan, farmer figures or compositions, or imply Dum E Odisha has the same farmer relationships. The earlier [illustrative-directions research](illustrative-directions-research.md) retains the fuller context.

## Common guide and evaluation requirements

Each route needs its own original illustration, complete **Dum E Odisha** name, visible separation around **E**, individual palette, type direction, secondary graphic language and packaging demonstration. Use **Flavour in Every Layer.** as the common tagline, below the name when there is enough room. Display the full mark and a symbol-only view. Decorative storytelling belongs outside the small logo when necessary. Every concept is generated through Nano Banana; do not describe raster output as vector masters, manufacture-ready packaging or measured geometric construction. Existing concepts remain accessible as history. Evaluate first by recognisable silhouette, cooking/hospitality meaning, name legibility and difference between routes; attractive mockups do not compensate for an unclear symbol.

The references were read through indexed primary-source text. Several direct Odisha government opens timed out; their indexed official text was available. This is not an exhaustive visual or historical survey, an artisan-authorship claim, or a trademark search.
