# A meal told through a symbol

This exploration puts the illustration before the lettering. A recognisable human gesture, generous vessel or growing botanical form carries the story; the full name signs it. Shapes should be bold enough to read as silhouettes and meaningful enough to explain in one sentence.

The Shared Handi centres on two people and one meal. Curved arms, a rounded vessel and rising rice suggest generosity, company and nourishment. The wider packaging can tell a contemporary story of growing, preparing and sharing food through spare human and plant forms. This is an original folk-inspired design, not a ritual Saura painting or an artisan-made claim.

The Dum Garden treats aroma as a growing botanical composition. A balanced pair of stems and a handi unite flavour and preparation. Mughal botanical art is the historical reference for flower-and-leaf rhythm; the brand does not claim a royal recipe or historical lineage. Broad silhouettes replace miniature-painting detail.

The Dum Keeper makes care visible through the gesture of holding and smelling a handi. Face, arm and pot use negative space to make one compact symbol. It should feel welcoming without becoming a generic chef caricature.

Across all routes, DUM and E remain separate words. ODISHA stays part of the full name. The tagline, Flavour in Every Layer., belongs in supporting application layouts. Distinct palettes and illustration grammars should make these genuinely different identity systems rather than recolours of the same logo.
