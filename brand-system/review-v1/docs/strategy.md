# Strategy — review v1, hypothesis to validate
## Recommended frame
Dum E Odisha is a biryani brand. Make the full name easy to find and the food easy to choose. Retain “Flavour in Every Layer.” as the existing brand line, not as evidence of a specific recipe or a superiority claim.

Working audience hypothesis: people comparing biryani options on small screens or reading a takeaway label. This follows existing applications, not demographic research. Prioritize legible names, portions and clear order information over fine ornament. Do not target tourists, families, students or price segments without evidence.

Proposed positioning: **A warm, straightforward biryani identity built around the anticipation of opening the lid.** This is a brand expression, not a statement about cooking methods or actual product quality. Reasons to believe must come from verified dishes, preparation practice and service performance; none are invented here.

Two strategic options: (A) clear everyday food choice — broad, useful, less distinctive without consistent execution; (B) Odisha craft storytelling — potentially richer local connection, but needs cultural participation, factual recipe context and owner intent. Recommend A for this review because it is compatible with the existing lid/name and does not borrow cultural authority. Keep Odisha in the name without equating it to a traditional regional recipe.

Values as behaviors: clarity means write portion and price plainly once verified; care means preserve allergens and order details instead of sacrificing them to decorative type; warmth means short invitations and respectful recovery messages, not exaggerated promises.

Tradeoffs: avoid fine craft ornament in the primary logo; avoid discount-first positioning and premium/heritage claims until the business chooses and substantiates them. Do not create separate brands for menu variants. Use one master brand, with dish names as information.

Disconfirmation plan: owner supplies actual menu, ordering context and three common customer questions; observe five real people finding the name and a dish on a phone-size application (with consent, no invented results). If regional craft is central to the operating business, revisit strategy with local collaborators before developing cultural motifs. If labels are not used operationally, reprioritize applications.
